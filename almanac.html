FORTUNA ALMANAC

REFERENCE / CURRENT TABLES
The Fortometer reports the amount and behavior of usable fortune in the surrounding day. It is read alongside lunar position, seasonal carry, and the Standard Correction. Atmospheric pressure concerns air. Fortune Pressure concerns fortune.

Fortune Pressure

Fortune Pressure is the ordinary measurement used to describe how firmly fortune is attached to a date. Higher readings are not necessarily better. Lower readings are not necessarily worse. Direction must be considered before the reading can be used.

Pressure is recorded in fHg, the traditional unit used in Fortuna tables. Conversion to ordinary pressure is discouraged because the values are not compatible.

A rising reading indicates that unused fortune is moving toward the present date. A falling reading means that usable fortune is settling behind it. Steady pressure means the difference is too small to be useful without consulting Carry.

Lunar Correction

Fortune tends to arrive early during a waxing moon and late during a waning moon. Lunar Correction keeps those differences from being mistaken for ordinary pressure change.

Correction is measured from the nearest lunar shoulder, rather than from visible phase. A lunar shoulder is the three-day region surrounding a major change in moon behavior. The shoulder may begin a day early when pressure is already falling.

This is why two evenings with nearly identical moons can produce different Balance strain. The moon may look the same while belonging to a different shoulder.

Seasonal Carry

Seasonal Carry is fortune remaining from the preceding portion of the year. Carry generally travels west through the warmer half of the year and east through the colder half. Transitional months should be read according to local custom.

Carry does not create fortune. It changes where fortune is considered to be waiting. On western days, reversals made late in a reading tend to hold better than reversals made early. Eastern days show the opposite tendency except on late-month Tuesdays.

A change in Carry is not itself a condition change. It merely changes what the same pressure is allowed to mean.

The Standard Correction

The Standard Correction is applied after ordinary pressure has been calculated. It accounts for known discrepancy between calculated fortune and fortune as it actually occurred.

The current correction method was adopted in 1987 and supersedes the 1964 tables. Readings made under the earlier tables should not be compared directly with modern fHg values.

Daily Conditions

A condition is not a second measurement. It is the ordinary name given to the combined pressure, direction, carry, lunar position, and correction present at the start of the day.

CLEARCorrections generally hold.
FAIRMost reversals behave as expected.
VARIABLESmall changes may carry farther than they look.
UNSETTLEDThe first apparent balance should not be trusted.
HEAVYReversals may carry unusual weight.
CROSSEDOpposing values are likely to resemble one another.
SEVEREOrdinary judgment may be unreliable.
STILLEverything appears to be fine.

Common Questions

Why did my condition change while pressure stayed the same?
Conditions use direction and surrounding tendency in addition to pressure.
Yesterday was FAIR at 29.18 fHg. Why is today VARIABLE at 29.18 fHg?
Yesterday was likely rising.
Yesterday was falling.
Then the difference was probably seasonal.
What does fHg stand for?
fHg is the traditional unit used for Fortune Pressure.
Can I calculate tomorrow's condition myself?
Yes.
Should Fortune Pressure be converted to ordinary pressure?
No. The values are not compatible.
